# Narrative manuscript

All text below is proposed finished player-facing copy, except explicitly labelled authoring directions. Paragraph breaks are reading pages where useful; titles are journal headings. No runtime change is made here. Area numbering is one-based for editors; existing area IDs remain unchanged.

## Part One — Carried Away

*A storm, a flood, and a very long way from home.*

### opening — The Night of the Flood

Sally's stone held the sun longer than any other stone in the garden.

Beneath it was her burrow. Beside it stood the old gate, with a hole just large enough for a lizard who knew how to turn sideways. Sally knew. She had worn the earth smooth going through it.

Then the rain came.

By midnight the flower beds were streams. By morning the streams had joined. Sally climbed onto her stone, but the water climbed higher.

The current took her over the wall.

She woke tangled in grass beside an old tortoise. There was mud in her ears and a leaf stuck to her nose.

"Don't get up too quickly," he said. "You might frighten the leaf."

Sally shook it off. "Where's the gate?"

The tortoise looked beyond the meadow.

"A long way off. I'm Old Moss. I used to carry messages through these gardens."

He gave her his small journal. On a blank leaf at the back, Sally drew her stone, the gate and the little hole beneath it.

"There," she said. "That's where I'm going."

Old Moss folded the paper carefully.

"Then we'd better keep that dry."

### area:meadow — Sunny Meadow

Beyond the grass, a snake lay coiled beside a rusty lantern hook.

"There used to be lights along every path," said Old Moss. "Glow bugs in little houses. You could follow them anywhere."

"What happened?"

"The paths broke. The keepers couldn't mend them. Now everyone guards the bit they know."

Sally tucked her drawing beneath a fold of dry leaf.

"Does everyone include him?"

The snake lifted its head.

"Very much him. Keep still in the grass, little one. Let him look somewhere else."

### milestone:small-kindness — The Other Bank

Authoring direction: safe overworld vignette after meadow progression; Sally nudges a loose twig, not a new escort objective.

A young snail waited beside a narrow wash of water. Her family were on the other side.

Sally could have stepped across. Instead she pushed a fallen twig until one end touched the bank.

The snail began to cross.

It took a very long time.

"You needn't wait," said Old Moss.

"I know," said Sally.

She waited until the small shell reached the far side. Then she put her drawing away and went on.

### area:garden — Garden Ruins

From Old Moss's journal:

*West beds. Mend the low wall. Replace the lantern bracket. Ask someone with smaller feet to inspect the narrow passage.*

Underneath, in different ink:

*Still not mended.*

Sally found the passage. A fallen stone had left a gap just wide enough for her shoulders.

On its underside was a little carved foot, pointing into the garden.

The stone felt warm.

She turned it over. The sun had not reached that side at all.

### area:river — Reedbanks

The river was smaller here. Sally did not trust it any better.

A frog sat on a post above the brown water. Around the post ran a row of carved feet, nearly worn away.

"Old road," he said.

"It's a river."

"Road first. River later. Things change."

On Old Moss's page, a pencil line crossed the same bend. It did not follow the bank. It passed straight beneath the water.

Sally touched the drawing. For a moment she thought she could feel where it went.

### area:ruins — Restless Ruins

Someone had built these doors to answer one another.

A plate pressed here, a passage opened there. A key lifted, an entrance shut. Beneath the grinding stone, Sally began to hear the order of it.

In the journal, Old Moss had copied a rubbing: four little feet around a broken circle.

*Wayfinder mark,* he had written. *A safe return is part of every journey.*

Sally looked back at the passage behind her.

She decided to remember more than the way in.

### area:quarry — Deep Trials

The steps were cut for small feet. Some had been worn hollow by generations of them.

Old Moss's journal called this a school.

*Not for being fearless. Fear is useful. It tells you to look again.*

Beside a drawing of two snakes, someone had added:

*Looked again. Still snakes. — F.*

Sally laughed, then stopped at the next corner and looked.

There were two.

### area:desert — Venom Depths

The sand held more heat than Sally wanted. She found herself missing the exact warmth of her own stone.

Here the lantern brackets were shaded with little clay roofs. Someone had thought about the glow bugs, even in this fierce place.

A scorpion raised its tail beside a broken roof.

Sally waited.

The tail lowered. She slipped past.

At the bottom of the journal page Old Moss had written:

*Must bring water next time. For the lanterns. For everyone.*

There had not been a next time.

### area:marsh — The Waking Maze

Roots had lifted the stones. Water had filled the tracks. The map and the ground disagreed at nearly every turn.

Yet Sally kept noticing where a path ought to be.

Not where it looked easiest. Somewhere underneath that.

Between two reeds she found another little carved foot. Its neighbour had fallen sideways into the mud.

Sally straightened the loose marker.

For an instant, a pale thread ran between them.

She held very still.

This time she knew she had seen it.

### area:jungle — Hollow Jungle

At the back of the journal, Old Moss had tucked a note.

*If you find a little footmark that answers you, follow it below the jungle. There is a stone there that will know more than I do.*

*Sally, When you woke, I saw an old mark warm beneath your feet. I think you may be able to feel the old paths. I cannot. I would rather tell you what I suspect than pretend I am certain.*

*The old travellers were called Wayfinders. I carried their messages.*

Below the letter lay a pressed leaf. Its stem pointed down.

Sally folded her drawing inside it and went looking for the way beneath the roots.

## Part Two — The Wayfinder

*The old paths have not quite forgotten.*

### area:glow — The Answering Stone

The stone beneath the jungle bore a lizard, carved in mid-stride.

Sally set one foot beside its little stone foot.

Light ran into the floor.

It spread through old marks in three directions. One thread reached a broken edge and stopped. Sally felt the missing connection as clearly as a gap beneath her toes.

She laid the journal's rubbing beside it. The broken circle was a map of this very place.

The Wayfinders had not merely known the paths. They could feel when the paths joined.

Sally could feel them too.

She was still muddy. She was still a long way from home.

But now she knew what the warm stones had been asking.

### area:orchard — Living Labyrinth

The first field sheet waited in a dry hollow, wrapped in bark.

It was crowded with roots, lantern marks and tiny corrections. A note at the edge read:

*If the hedge grows across the route, redraw the route. The hedge cannot read.*

A smaller path curved away from the road Sally wanted. Beside it someone had drawn three snail shells and a lantern.

The page ended at a break in the line.

Sally knew what that break felt like.

### milestone:carry-the-light — The Longer Way

Authoring direction: after Living Labyrinth progression, before entering Sun and Sea; fixed narrative choice, no extra completion condition.

Near the broken mark, a snail family had gathered beneath a leaf. Their feeding beds lay beyond the dark orchard. They would not risk their young on a path they could no longer follow.

Sally unfolded her drawing. The road towards her gate was plain enough now.

Then she looked at the little shells on the field sheet.

She turned back through the orchard, following the old marks until the broken thread met its neighbour.

A low light held between them. It did not travel far. It was enough to show the next bend.

When Sally returned, the leaf shelter was empty. A trail of silver crossed the roots.

She put the field sheet beside her drawing.

There was room for both.

### area:coast — Sun and Sea

At the coast, the map became a collection of careful guesses.

Safe at low water. Check after storms. Ask the crabs.

The crabs had once carried lantern oilcloth above the tide. One still sheltered beneath a scrap of it, holding the corner down with a claw.

Beyond him, an old route ended at the sea.

An arrow pointed out from the shore. Alongside it someone had written:

*Outer beacon silent. Inspection party crossing. Local keepers have the sheets. Expect us back before the stores run low.*

Sally looked at the weathered ink.

It had been a long wait.

### area:hollow — The Shedding Hollow

Old skins hung in the dry branches, fine as empty sleeves.

Sally found a patch of sunlight and stayed in it longer than she meant to.

On this sheet a Wayfinder had drawn a tail twice: once whole, once short.

*Lost it at the north wall. Miss it terribly. Still glad it wasn't the rest of me.*

Below that, added later:

*Growing back crooked. Good enough.*

Sally stretched her toes in the sun.

The people who had made this map had been frightened sometimes. And tired. That helped.

### area:lantern — The Lantern Marsh

A hundred empty hooks stood above the water.

Wick, the beetle who kept the courtyard, had saved one lantern in a hollow stump. Its glow bugs were alive. They showed small green sparks beneath the lid.

"They haven't forgotten," he said. "But the path won't hold their light."

Sally put the field sheets together. The marks almost joined.

Almost.

In the distance an owl turned on an empty hook.

"I'll see where it breaks," she said.

### milestone:one-lantern — A Light That Stays

Authoring direction: after Lantern Marsh progression; show only a sheltered overworld lantern, never remove an active level hazard.

The final mark was under a root.

When Sally stepped beyond it, the thread behind her stopped flickering.

Wick lifted the lid of the lantern. Light gathered in the glass and reached the first hook, then the next. At the third it faded.

"It didn't go all the way," said Sally.

"No," said Wick.

He set the lantern on its bracket.

They watched it together.

It stayed alight.

### area:rooftops — Sunbaked Rooftops

Up here the wind tried to take every loose thing Sally owned.

She tucked the field sheets inside the old leaf, then tucked the leaf beneath her chin.

Across one page, a route had been crossed out and drawn again beneath the awnings.

*Fennel took the ridge,* someone had written. *Arrived first. Lost the lunch.*

Underneath:

*Recovered most of it. — Fennel*

A little feather was caught in the fold.

Sally chose the awnings.

### area:ants — Ant Kingdom

The ants' copy of the map was almost perfect.

Every turn was measured. Every store had a mark. Every mark had a date.

Sally found her own route drawn through the tunnels in faint old ink. The ants had kept it clear long after the lanterns failed.

An ant touched the page with one antenna, then touched the warm mark beneath Sally's foot.

For a moment, neither moved.

Then the ant set down a fresh crumb beside her.

Sally had been counting pages. Someone else had been keeping the paths.

## Part Three — The Great Map

*There is still a way through.*

### area:frost — Frost Hollow

The sentinels stood where the map said they would.

Ice had gathered on their shoulders. Their gaze passed across the frozen walls, searching the old crossings.

Beside a drawing of one sentinel, a Wayfinder had written:

*They will hold their posts until we return. Leave the warm stones clear for them.*

The next line ran beneath a stain:

*If the outer beacon cannot be mended, we must find a way to…*

The rest was gone.

Sally rested one foot on a warm stone.

Someone had expected to finish that sentence.

### milestone:the-wait — Still Here

Authoring direction: safe post-area vignette when Steam Vents becomes accessible; moving/hostile sentinels in replayable levels remain unchanged.

At the last waymark, light showed briefly beneath the ice.

A sentinel in the courtyard lowered its head towards it.

Sally waited for a question. None came.

"I don't know where they are," she said.

The sentinel did not move.

"But this way works again."

She laid the corrected sheet on the warm stone long enough for the ice to dry from its edge.

When she went on, the sentinel was looking down the path she had opened.

### area:vents — Steam Vents

The pipes had once warmed glasshouses. Now steam escaped wherever a joint had loosened.

Rivet had spent the morning repairing a valve with wire. Being a snail made it slow work. Having no hands made it slower.

"They left instructions," he said. "Good ones."

He nudged a stack of stained maintenance sheets.

"Instructions don't tighten things."

Sally held the wire steady while he worked.

When the hissing eased, she could hear a second pipe farther on.

There was still work to do.

### area:grotto — Mushroom Grotto

The grotto had kept its own light.

It grew in the gills of mushrooms and the damp seams of the stone. No lantern had brought it here.

At a sheltered pool, Sally spread the map to dry. Other paws had corrected it before hers: beetle scratches, an ant's precise dots, Old Moss's wide, sloping hand.

She added the route she had walked through the marsh.

Her line was not very straight.

It met the next one.

She left it as it was.

### area:storm — Storm Heights

The first crack of thunder sent Sally beneath a stone ledge.

For a moment she was back on her warm stone, with the water rising.

She pressed her feet against the dry rock and counted the space before the next flash.

On the field sheet, a keeper had marked the ledges where a small traveller could wait.

Sally added the one she had found.

Then she folded the page, checked the next crossing and went out when she was ready.

### area:tide — Tide Pools

From the rocks, Sally could see her garden wall.

It was smaller than she remembered. The gate leaned just as it always had.

She nearly put the map away.

Then a thin line on the final field sheet caught her eye. It ran from the lantern marsh, through the tunnels, beneath the warm stones and back towards the gate.

The oldest circuit.

At its near end was a blank space, worn soft by many folds.

Sally took out her first drawing.

The gate fitted beside the line.

### area:home — The Road Home

Her drawing had been wrong about the size of the gate.

It had been right about the hole beneath it.

Sally smoothed the little page against the last field sheet. There was her stone, with the oldest lantern bracket beside it. There was the route leading away.

She had drawn where she belonged.

Now she needed to join it to everywhere she had been.

Beyond the gate, the stone was waiting.

### finale — Home

Sally turned sideways and slipped through the hole beneath the gate.

Her stone was still there.

She spread the map beside it and drew the last small line: from the path, beneath the gate, to the lantern bracket.

Then she walked it.

Light gathered in the old marks. It passed beneath her feet, through the gate and out beyond the wall.

Across the garden, lanterns began to answer.

Some had keepers waiting beside them. Others would need new glass or a handful of glow bugs. But the paths between them held.

Old Moss arrived as the sun reached the stone.

"You took the long way," he said.

Sally looked at the crowded map.

"Somebody needed it."

At the wall, a little snail crossed a twig laid over the drainage groove. Beyond her, the next lantern shone.

Sally climbed onto her stone. There was room at the edge for the map, weighted down against the wind.

This time, when she closed her eyes, she knew where the paths went.

### after — Beyond the Last Line

Authoring direction: voluntary separate interaction after the ending, never a completion requirement.

A few mornings later, Shelly brought something the tide had left in a bottle.

It was a map page.

The coast on it was unfamiliar. Beyond the shore stood a lantern, drawn in ink that had run a little. Beside it were small lizard footprints.

Someone had marked the route after arriving.

Old Moss laid the page beside the others.

"That isn't one of ours," he said.

Sally looked towards the sea.

For now, her stone was warm. The new page could dry beside it.

*The end of the first journey.*

### coast:landing — The Far Coast

Authoring direction: earned by winning Bottle Beach, the first escape on the Far Coast (opens once Home is cleared). Episode 2's first page.

The causeway ended on soft, cold sand.

The footprints from the map page were real. They were smaller than Sally's, and they went up the beach without stopping.

Someone had come ashore here and known exactly where they were going.

On the cliff above, the lantern from the page stood dark.

Sally tucked the map page into her pouch and followed the footprints up the path.

### note:far-coast — To Be Continued

The lantern at the top of the cliff was cold. Someone had cleaned the glass not long ago, and left the wick trimmed and ready.

Beside it, scratched into the stone, was a Wayfinder's mark, and an arrow pointing out to sea, towards an island Sally could only just make out.

The footprints went back down to the water's edge, and stopped.

Sally sat by the lantern until the light went, watching the island.

"Not yet," she said, to nobody in particular. "But soon."

## Existing secret victory cards — complete replacement text

Authoring direction: retain current `note:<spot>` IDs and secret-win triggers. These are not the physical level notes in LEVEL-NOTES.md. Each fragment stands alone; even the wreck confirms only a planned departure and repair, not arrival or survival. Signatures are part of the text.

### note:star-garden — Something Bright

I planted these beds when the western beacon first went dark. A lantern needs a keeper, but a flower is willing to do a little work by itself.

If the seeds have taken, tread between the rows. If they haven't, please admire the intention.

— F.

### note:mossy-well — A Good Listener

I told the well where I was going. It repeated every word.

Old Moss says that does not count as leaving a message with someone responsible.

I have left one with him too.

— F.

### note:cloud-nest — Farther Than the Garden

From here the sea looks small enough to cross before breakfast.

I know that is the height playing tricks. I have written it down so that, when I am in the boat, I cannot claim nobody warned me.

— F.

### note:challenge-shrine — Twice

Every Wayfinder walks the trials once. I walked them twice because I thought that would make me ready for anything.

The second time I fell in the same pool.

I am ready for that pool now.

— F.

### note:bestiary-grove — A Friend I Never Saw

A chameleon lives here. I have never seen him.

Each morning there is a beetle on my pillow. Each evening I leave a berry on his branch.

Neither of us has asked the other to explain.

— F.

### note:ice-cave — A Promise

I told the sentinels we would return. One lowered its head, as though it had put my words somewhere safe.

It is a strange feeling, being trusted by something that can wait longer than you can imagine.

— F.

### note:sunken-wreck — The First Launch

The first boat struck the rocks before we cleared the cove. Everyone got ashore. Most of the lunch did not.

We have moved the sound planks to higher ground. Next launch: wait for the full tide. Check every seam. Bring more string.

— Fennel, Wayfinder

### note:magpie-roost — Borrowed Shine

We used to leave spare pebbles here. The magpies called it their collection. We called it our stores.

An arrangement satisfactory to everyone, until somebody counted.

Take only what you can use. They notice the shiny things first.

— F.

### note:blind-grove — Looking Properly

I practised here until I stopped staring at my own feet.

A snake tells you where it will look next. The turn begins in its head. I had been watching the path so carefully that I kept missing the snake.

— F.

### note:gilded — The Wrong Treasure

Counted the gold. Counted it again. Tried holding a piece under the lantern hook.

Still dark.

I have left the gold where it is and gone to find the keeper.

— F.

### note:bramble — An Expert Opinion

Lost for three days.

Before you laugh: the hedge has grown since the map was drawn. After you laugh: please correct the map on your way out.

I would do it myself, but I am not yet entirely certain which way that is.

— F.
