# Four main-theme directions

## 1. Mysterious folk adventure — selected lead direction

Instrumental main theme for a small lizard exploring an ancient, overgrown world. Earthy, curious, slightly wild, with quiet mystery and a strong sense of adventure. 92 BPM, gently lilting 6/8. Fingerpicked acoustic guitar, hammered dulcimer, low wooden flute, bowed cello, warm double bass, and sparse frame drum. An instantly recognizable four-bar melody in a modal minor key, answered by a brighter phrase that suggests possibility. Intimate, tactile, organic sound; occasional rustling percussion. Memorable enough to become the game's recurring theme, restrained enough for an exploration map. No vocals, tropical resort feeling, jaunty cartoon bounce, big orchestral swells, or dramatic drops. Keep a steady energy, a short introduction, and an ending that returns naturally to the opening.

## 2. Atmospheric electronic exploration

Instrumental main theme for an exploratory adventure game set in a strange living wilderness. Spacious, intriguing, quietly confident. 88 BPM, 4/4. Warm analog synth arpeggios, soft rounded bass, delicate glass-like tones, airy pads, and a restrained broken beat. One memorable melodic motif, with subtle harmonic changes that suggest hidden places and unexplored paths. A modern, immersive sound with room for environmental effects; gentle forward motion rather than dance-floor energy. No vocals, aggressive bass, EDM drops, sentimental piano, or cinematic crescendos. Maintain consistent loudness and end with a phrase that can loop naturally.

## 3. Dark storybook adventure

Instrumental main theme for a mischievous lizard adventure through enchanted ruins and secret forests. Magical, curious, a little dangerous; adventurous rather than frightening. 100 BPM, 3/4. Pizzicato strings, bass clarinet, celesta, solo violin, and restrained hand percussion. A distinctive winding melody with playful pauses and a shadowy modal harmony, opening into a warmer, hopeful refrain. Small chamber ensemble, clear melody, plenty of space. No vocals, bombastic fantasy orchestra, horror stingers, sentimental ballad, or huge finale. Keep the arrangement light enough for a world map, with a clean return to the opening phrase.

## 4. Catchy retro adventure

Instrumental main theme for a top-down lizard exploration game. Catchy, adventurous, curious, with a little mischief. 112 BPM, 4/4. Expressive 16-bit-style lead synth, short plucked synth chords, rounded bass, soft electronic drums, and subtle atmospheric pads. A strong singable eight-bar melody with a contrasting discovery section; classic adventure-game energy with a clean modern mix. Make it feel like setting out into a world full of secrets, without sounding like a tropical bonus stage. No vocals, harsh square-wave overload, frantic arcade combat, EDM drops, or long introduction. Keep steady energy and finish on a phrase that loops into the beginning.
